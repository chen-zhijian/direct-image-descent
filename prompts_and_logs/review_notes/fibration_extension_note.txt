1. Theorem 7.1: a quantitative fibration extension

Let pi:(X,omega_X)->(Y,omega_Y) be a smooth proper surjective
holomorphic submersion, with complete Kahler metrics and positive
auxiliary holomorphic Hermitian line bundles on X and Y. Let (L,h)
be an arbitrary smooth Hermitian holomorphic line bundle. Assume
locally constant fiber cohomology dimension and use its ordinary
fiberwise harmonic metric H on F=R^q pi_*(Omega^p_(X/Y) tensor L).

In either extremal range of (1.2), total optimal (a+p,b+q) estimates
imply optimal (a,b) estimates for H under the pointwise condition

  mu + e >= delta + 2 m^2 (tau + sigma + kappa).

Here mu is the minimum eigenvalue over a fiber of the total curvature
commutator; e is the least fiber Bochner-energy eigenvalue on the
harmonic space. The costs are:

- kappa: squared Hilbert-Schmidt norms of the nonharmonic parts of
  the type-preserving horizontal covariant Lie derivatives;
- sigma: squared Hilbert-Schmidt norms of the actual Kodaira--Spencer
  and conjugate Kodaira--Spencer insertions on harmonic forms;
- tau: the tensor norm of the harmonic compression of the covariant
  Lie derivative along brackets of horizontal lifts;
- delta: the operator norm of the horizontal curvature commutator
  discrepancy after comparing the horizontal metric with omega_Y
  by the positive inverse-square-root isometry.

All quantities are defined from coefficient curvature, the fibration,
and vertical harmonic data; the hypothesis does not assume positivity
of the final direct-image metric.

The proof separates three steps: pointwise isometric compression of
total curvature and the compact-fiber Bochner identity; the established
Choi--Schumacher curvature formula plus Gauss and operator-norm bounds;
and the complete-Kahler estimate on the base. It does not claim that
the naive harmonic lift is closed in arbitrary varying families.
Green operators give an additional spectral bound for kappa.

If the horizontal metric equals pi^*omega_Y, delta=tau=0. The sufficient
condition becomes mu+e >= 2 m^2(sigma+kappa). On a fixed product the
Kodaira--Spencer tensor is zero, recovering the quantitative condition
of Theorem 6.1. If total curvature nonnegativity is assumed directly,
completeness and a positive auxiliary line are not needed on X.

2. Theorem 7.3: an exact top-degree lifting theorem

Assume q=n, b=m, an adapted horizontal metric as above, and pointwise
annihilation A_i contraction s=0 for every local holomorphic section
s of pi_*(Omega^(n-p)_(X/Y) tensor L^*). This requires pointwise
annihilation, rather than only vanishing of the insertion classes or
harmonic projections. It does NOT require the Kodaira--Spencer class
itself to vanish. In particular, annihilation is automatic when p=n,
including in nonisotrivial families.

Then total optimal (a+p,m+n) implies optimal (a,m), without a smallness
bound on harmonic variation. The orthogonal absolute extensions of
these Serre-dual sections are holomorphic. Fiber integration against
them defines a contractive chain projection on maximal L2 domains.
The harmonic lift of a top-base-degree datum is automatically closed;
the adapted metric gives exact inverse-energy equality. Solve upstairs
and apply the chain projection to obtain the constant-one estimate.

3. Nonisotrivial example and limitation

A varying elliptic family over a disk has the closed relative form
Omega=i d dbar ((Im z)^2 / Im t). With a complete base metric, adding
its pullback gives an adapted complete Kahler metric and nonzero
Kodaira--Spencer tensor. Flat coefficients and p=q=1 give the flat
trace direct image; all costs vanish. No coefficient pullback twist
is added. A positive auxiliary line exists independently.

On the same family, flat coefficients with p=0,q=1 satisfy the total
optimal (1,2) condition, but R^1 pi_*O has metric proportional to
(Im t)^(-1) and strictly negative curvature. Thus the unconditional
part of Theorem 6.1 does not extend to arbitrary varying fibrations.

4. Attribution and verification

The curvature identity is explicitly attributed to Choi--Schumacher,
formula (7.2), with the bracket geometry from their Lemma 8.1. The
optimal-estimate characterization is attributed to Watanabe. The
sufficient conditions are not claimed necessary, sharp, or a new
general direct-image positivity theorem. The distinct prescribed
metrics in other higher-direct-image results remain distinguished.
